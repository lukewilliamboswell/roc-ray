[.lead]
Make 2D games, visual tools, and other interactive programs in Roc. You write
the rules and say what to draw; RocRay opens the window, reads the keyboard
and mouse, plays sound, and runs your code many times a second. This manual
assumes no Roc and no graphics experience. It takes you from installing the
compiler to a small finished game, and then through each feature as your own
app needs it.

== Choose a path

New to RocRay:: Follow these in order.
+
. <<overview>>: what RocRay is for, and how an app is put together.
. <<install>> and <<run-an-example>>: set up the compiler and see an app run.
. <<roc-primer>>: the Roc you need, if the language is new to you.
. <<smallest-app>>: write and run your first program.
. <<first-game>>: grow it, step by step, into a small game.
. <<app-model>>, then the other guides as your app needs them.
. <<shipping>>: give your finished app to other people.
+
<<glossary>> explains any unfamiliar term along the way.

Coming from raylib:: Read <<overview>>, then <<coming-from-raylib>>, which maps
raylib's functions and habits onto RocRay. <<roc-primer>> covers the language.
Building an app:: Read <<app-model>> first; every later guide builds on it.
Then read the guides for the features you need, in any order: <<input>>,
<<drawing>>, <<tasks>>, and so on. Before your app touches a file, the
network, or another program, read <<permissions>>. <<shipping>> covers giving
your app to other people.
Testing and investigating performance:: <<testing>> covers pure tests and
unattended runs. <<observatory>> records what the host did, cycle by cycle,
into a database you can query.
Curious about the design:: <<architecture>> states the rules the guides
introduce one at a time, and why RocRay chose them.
Contributing to RocRay:: Start with <<contributing>>. <<host-development>>
covers changes to the native host, and <<maintaining>> covers dependencies,
bundles, and releases.
